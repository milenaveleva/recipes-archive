## Method

1. **Preparation:** Remove the outer, damaged leaves of the Brussels sprouts, trim the stems and cut them in half (if using frozen, let them thaw slightly). Chop the onion and finely grate the garlic and ginger. Drain the chickpeas and rinse them thoroughly with cold water.
2. Cook the brown basmati rice in about 2-2.25 times its volume of lightly salted water for 30-40 minutes, then leave it to steam covered for 10 minutes until serving.
3. **Searing and cooking:** In a deep, wide pan, heat 1 tablespoon of coconut oil. Place the halved Brussels sprouts in it cut side down and sear over medium-high heat for 5-7 minutes without shaking until a nice golden-brown, caramelised crust forms on them. Remove the sprouts to a plate.
4. In the same pan, add the remaining 1 tablespoon of coconut oil, then sauté the chopped onion in it until translucent. Add the grated garlic and ginger and fry for 1 minute until fragrant. Sprinkle in the garam masala, ground cumin and turmeric, then toast for another 30 seconds.
5. Pour in the chopped tomatoes and coconut milk. Stir in the rinsed chickpeas, the seared Brussels sprouts and the fine salt. Bring to a boil, then simmer on low heat, half covered, for 12-15 minutes until the sauce thickens creamy and the sprouts are tender.
6. **Serving:** Taste the curry and adjust the seasoning if necessary with a little salt or a pinch of garam masala. Serve in deep plates alongside the steamed brown basmati rice, sprinkle generously with fresh coriander, and serve hot drizzled with fresh lime juice.

## Tips

- Searing the Brussels sprouts first is an essential step: through caramelisation they lose their bitter taste and gain a pleasantly nutty, sweet aroma that goes fantastically with Indian coconut curry.
- The curry is even more flavourful the next day as the flavours meld. In a closed container in the fridge it stays fresh for 3-4 days, so it's a great idea for cooking ahead.
