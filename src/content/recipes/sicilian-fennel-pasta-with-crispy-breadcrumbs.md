## Method

1. **Prepare and make the breadcrumbs:** Soak the raisins in warm water for about 10 minutes, so that they soften nicely. Cut off the fennel fronds (the delicate, dill-like sprigs) and set them aside for serving, halve the bulbs, remove the core and slice them into hair-thin strips. Finely chop the onion and garlic.
2. Toast the pine nuts golden brown in a dry frying pan, then set aside on a plate. In the same pan pour 1 tablespoon of olive oil, add the breadcrumbs and a pinch of chili flakes, and toast over medium heat, stirring constantly, into golden, crunchy crumbs (the Sicilian "mollica atturrata"). Set aside to cool.
3. **Caramelise the fennel and toss the pasta:** In a large frying pan heat the remaining extra virgin olive oil. Add the chopped onion and the thinly sliced fennel. Sweat over medium heat for 8–10 minutes, until the fennel is soft and starts to caramelise lightly. Then add the crushed garlic and the drained raisins, and season with salt and pepper to taste.
4. Pour the vegetable broth and a squeeze of lemon juice into the pan and simmer for 2–3 minutes, until the fennel forms a silky, aromatic base.
5. Meanwhile, boil plenty of salted water in a large pot and cook the spaghetti until al dente. Drain the pasta, but keep 1 ladleful (about 80 ml) of the pasta cooking water.
6. Toss the hot pasta directly into the fennel sauce in the pan. Pour in the reserved pasta cooking water, grate in the zest of the organic lemon and squeeze in its juice. Toss over high heat for 1–2 minutes, so that the silky emulsion coats the strands perfectly.
7. Serve in deep plates: sprinkle with the toasted pine nuts, the fresh fennel fronds and the crunchy, spiced Sicilian breadcrumbs.

## Tips

- In Sicilian cooking, toasted spiced breadcrumbs (mollica atturrata) are the "poor man's Parmesan", which gives pasta dishes an irresistible crunchy texture.
- If you like extra spicy flavours, feel free to add a few anchovies to the sauce when heating the oil for an even richer Mediterranean taste.
