## Method

1. **Preparation and toasting:** Break the dry spaghetti by hand into pieces about 2-3 cm long (this gives fideuà its characteristic short noodles). Rinse the sea bass fillet, pat it completely dry with paper towels, then cut into 3-4 cm bite-sized pieces. Season lightly with salt and pepper.
2. Finely dice the onion and the kápia pepper. Crush the garlic cloves and chop finely (set a little aside for the aioli). Heat the vegetable broth in a small pot.
3. In a large, wide paella or cast-iron pan, heat 2 tablespoons of olive oil. Add the sea bass pieces and sear quickly for 1.5-2 minutes until they form a nice golden crust but the flesh stays tender inside. Remove to a clean plate and keep warm.
4. **The sofrito and cooking the fideuà:** Add another 1 tablespoon of olive oil to the fat remaining in the pan, add the broken dry spaghetti, and toast over medium heat, stirring constantly, for 3-4 minutes until golden brown and nutty-smelling, then transfer to a bowl.
5. Pour the remaining olive oil into the pan and sweat the chopped onion and kápia pepper for 5-6 minutes until soft. Stir in most of the chopped garlic and the smoked paprika, fry for 30 seconds, then stir in the diced tomatoes. Cook for 4-5 minutes until you have a thick sofrito base.
6. Return the toasted pasta to the sofrito so every piece is coated in the flavourful sauce. Pour in the hot vegetable broth, season to taste with salt and pepper, and shake the pan to distribute evenly. Cook over moderate heat for 8-10 minutes until the pasta has absorbed most of the liquid and stands upright in the pan.
7. **Finishing and serving:** In the last 3 minutes of cooking, place the pre-seared sea bass pieces on top of the pan, gently pressing them into the pasta so they warm through and steam until buttery soft. Leave the pan for another minute without moving so the bottom crisps and forms the famous crunchy 'socarrat' pasta crust.
8. For the quick homemade aioli, mix the mayonnaise with the reserved crushed garlic and a few drops of fresh lemon juice. Sprinkle the fideuà with freshly chopped parsley and serve straight from the hot pan with lemon wedges and the creamy garlic aioli.

## Tips

- The essence of fideuà is thoroughly toasting the dry pasta in olive oil: this gives it a nutty, full aroma and keeps it springy and firm instead of getting soggy while cooking.
- Don't stir the pasta after pouring in the broth! Just shake the pan, so the strands on the bottom caramelise to a crisp (this is the pinnacle of Spanish cooking, the 'socarrat').
