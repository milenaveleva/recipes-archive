## Method

1. Cut the butter into small cubes and return it to the fridge to keep cold. Chop the shallot finely.
2. Bring 1.2 litres of water to the boil, then remove from the heat and keep warm.
3. From the trimmed asparagus, cut off the 4 cm tips and set aside. Cut 2 cm of the woody base off the spears and discard. Cut the remaining stalks into 0.5 cm rounds.
4. In a saucepan, heat 3 tablespoons of olive oil over medium heat. Add the shallot and cook, stirring occasionally, until translucent.
5. Add the rice and fry for 2 minutes, stirring frequently. Then pour in all the wine at once and let it evaporate completely, stirring constantly.
6. Reduce the heat, add the sliced asparagus, season lightly with salt and pour in about 100 ml hot water (just enough to cover the rice). Stir frequently and let the water be almost completely absorbed.
7. When the water has almost gone, add another portion of water, again just enough to cover. Continue cooking this way until the rice is al dente (soft outside, slightly firm inside). This usually takes 16-20 minutes. Stir very often!
8. When half of the last portion of water has evaporated and the rice has the right consistency, take it off the heat. Stir in the Parmesan, then the cold butter cubes in batches, and finally 3 tablespoons of olive oil.
9. If the risotto is too thick, loosen it with a little hot water. Stir until you get a creamy emulsion. Season with salt and pepper to taste and let it rest for 1 minute. Stir again before serving.
10. Halve the reserved asparagus tips lengthways. In a frying pan, heat 2 tablespoons of olive oil over high heat, add the asparagus tips and fry, stirring constantly, for 1.5 minutes. Remove from the heat, season with a pinch of salt and pepper and use them to garnish the risotto portions.

## Tips

- When making risotto, do not brown the onion, only sweat it, or its flavour will be too dominant. As soon as you smell the onion, you can add the rice.
- Once you add the cheese and butter, do not cook the dish any further, or the risotto will be greasy instead of forming a creamy emulsion.
- You can use a mild vegetable stock instead of water, but make sure it does not overpower the delicate flavour of the asparagus. In that case, simmer the woody asparagus ends in the stock for 10 minutes, then strain.
