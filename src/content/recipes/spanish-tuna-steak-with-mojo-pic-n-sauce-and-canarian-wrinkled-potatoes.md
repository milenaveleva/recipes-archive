## Method

1. **Making the salted wrinkled potatoes (Papas arrugadas):** Wash the new potatoes thoroughly but do not peel them. Put them in a saucepan, add just enough water to cover and add 1 teaspoon of salt. Cook over medium heat for 15–20 minutes until the potatoes are completely soft. Pour off the water, then put the pan back on the hot stove over low heat. Shake gently for 1–2 minutes until the moisture has completely evaporated, the characteristic white salt layer of the Canaries appears on the potato skins and the skins become wrinkled.
2. **Making the Canarian red mojo picón sauce:** Wash the kápia peppers and the red chilli, remove the stems and chop coarsely (if you want milder heat, remove the chilli's ribs and seeds). Peel the garlic cloves. Put the chopped kápia peppers, chilli, garlic, smoked paprika, ground cumin, white wine vinegar, half a teaspoon of salt and 40 ml of extra virgin olive oil in a chopper. Blend until creamy, but keep a nice rustic texture.
3. **Pan-searing the tuna steak and serving:** Pat the fresh tuna steaks thoroughly dry with a paper towel. Gently rub both sides with half a teaspoon of salt and freshly ground black pepper. Heat a frying pan until smoking with the remaining 20 ml of olive oil. Place the tuna steaks in the hot pan. Cook both sides over intense heat for exactly 1½–2 minutes so the outside gets a golden brown crust while the inside stays juicy and a nice pink (medium-rare). Slice the rested tuna on the diagonal. Serve with the hot, salt-crusted new potatoes, spoon a generous amount of the spicy mojo picón sauce alongside, sprinkle with fresh chopped parsley and serve with fresh lemon wedges.

## Tips

- The real secret of tuna steak is quick, hot searing: do not overcook it, keep it silky and pink inside, because overcooked tuna dries out easily.
- Mojo picón sauce keeps well in the fridge, sealed, for 4–5 days; it is a fantastic dip not only for fish, but also for roast chicken or vegetables.
