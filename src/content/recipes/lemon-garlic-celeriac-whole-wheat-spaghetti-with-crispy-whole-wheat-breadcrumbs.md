## Method

1. **Preparation and crispy crumbs:** Peel the celeriac, then cut into thin matchsticks (julienne) or thin ribbons with a vegetable peeler. Cut the garlic cloves into hair-thin slices. Finely grate the zest of the organic lemon and squeeze out its juice. Chop the parsley finely.
2. Make the crunchy crumbs (pangrattato): heat 20 ml of extra virgin olive oil in a pan over medium heat. Add the breadcrumbs and a pinch of salt and fry, stirring constantly, for 3-4 minutes until golden brown and crisp. Stir in half of the chopped parsley and half of the grated lemon zest, then take off the heat and set aside in a small bowl.
3. **Cooking the pasta and frying the celeriac:** Bring plenty of water to the boil in a large pot, salt it well (about 1 tsp fine salt), then add the dried spaghetti. Cook for 1 minute less than the time on the package, so it stays firm and perfectly al dente. Before draining, scoop out and set aside about 150-200 ml of starchy cooking water.
4. While the pasta cooks, in a larger, deep pan heat the remaining 40 ml of extra virgin olive oil plus the additional olive oil. Add the thinly sliced garlic and chili flakes and cook over low-medium heat for 1-2 minutes, making sure the garlic softens but does not brown.
5. Throw the prepared thin celeriac strips into the pan, season with freshly ground black pepper and fry over medium heat for 4-5 minutes until the celeriac gets a nice colour and softens but keeps a pleasant crunch.
6. **Assembly and serving:** Tip the cooked spaghetti directly into the pan with the celeriac. Drizzle with the freshly squeezed lemon juice and the other half of the reserved lemon zest and add about 100 ml of the reserved hot cooking water. Boil over high heat for 1-2 minutes, tossing and shaking continuously, until the olive oil and starchy water form a glossy, silky emulsion around the strands.
7. Take off the heat, sprinkle with half of the freshly grated Parmesan and the remaining parsley, then toss thoroughly.
8. Serve the hot spaghetti in deep plates. Sprinkle generously over each portion with the reserved crispy breadcrumbs (pangrattato) and the remaining Parmesan. Eat immediately!

## Tips

- Cutting the celeriac into thin strips is key: julienne strips similar to the thickness of the spaghetti blend perfectly with the pasta and brown evenly in the garlic oil.
- The starchy pasta water is worth its weight in gold: it helps the olive oil emulsify, so the result is creamy and rich without a sauce and without using cream.
