## Method

1. Preheat the oven to 200 °C (400 °F, fan). Wash the red bell peppers, halve them, remove the seeds and cut into larger pieces. Place them skin side up on a baking tray lined with baking paper, together with the peeled garlic cloves. Drizzle with 15 ml extra virgin olive oil, sprinkle with a pinch of fine salt and roast for 20–25 minutes until the pepper flesh is soft and the skin is charred in places.
2. While the peppers roast, toast the pine nuts in a dry pan over low heat for 2–3 minutes until golden and fragrant, shaking the pan constantly. Set aside to cool.
3. Put a large pot of plenty of water on to boil for the pasta. When it boils, salt it with fine salt, add the dry fusilli and cook al dente as directed on the package. Before draining, scoop out 100–150 ml of the starchy cooking water.
4. Put the roasted peppers and garlic into a food processor or blender. Add the ricotta, the remaining 15 ml extra virgin olive oil, half of the chilli flakes, half of the Parmesan, half of the fresh basil leaves, and the remaining fine salt and ground black pepper. Blend into a velvety smooth, creamy sauce. If needed, add 50 ml warm pasta water to reach the desired consistency.
5. Pour the creamy pepper sauce over the hot, drained fusilli and toss thoroughly over low heat for 1 minute so the spiral ridges of the fusilli trap the sauce perfectly.
6. Serve the fusilli warm in deep plates. Garnish with the reserved toasted pine nuts, the remaining Parmesan, fresh basil leaves and a pinch of chilli flakes.

## Tips

- If you want to peel the peppers more easily, cover them in a bowl or with foil for 5 minutes while still warm after roasting; but blended with the skins on they also give the sauce a lovely full, sweet, silky flavour.
- The starchy pasta cooking water is a treasure in Italian cooking: always use it to adjust the silkiness of the pepper-ricotta sauce.
