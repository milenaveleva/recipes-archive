## Method

1. **Stewing and puréeing the quince:** Wash the quinces thoroughly and rub off the outer downy layer. Cut them into quarters, remove the cores and woody inner parts, then dice the flesh into 2–3 cm pieces. Leave the skin on for the wonderful flavour and pectin content. Put the quince cubes in a saucepan with the 150 ml of water, the freshly squeezed juice of half a lemon, the whole cloves and half of the cinnamon. Cook covered over low heat for about 20–25 minutes, until the quince is completely soft. Fish the cloves out of the pot. Purée the cooked fruit completely smooth with a hand blender, stir in the honey, then let it cool completely to room temperature, and finally chill it in the fridge for 20 minutes so it is cold.
2. **Assembling the mousse and serving:** While the quince purée cools, chop the walnuts coarsely and toast them in a dry pan over medium heat for 2–3 minutes until fragrant and golden brown, then set aside to cool. Whip the well-chilled plant-based whipping cream in a deep bowl with a hand mixer to a stiff, stable cream. With a spatula, fold the cold quince purée into the plant-based whipped cream with gentle, loose movements so the cream does not collapse, to get a light, silky mousse. Spoon the quince mousse into four elegant goblets or glasses. Sprinkle generously with the crisp toasted walnut pieces and the remaining cinnamon and serve immediately or after a short chill.

## Tips

- It is important that the quince purée cools completely before mixing it into the plant-based whipped cream, otherwise the heat would make the cream collapse and run.
- To make it even more special, you can crumble a few plain biscuits or ladyfingers into the bottom of the glass before layering the quince mousse.
