## Method

1. Press the tofu well with a clean tea towel or paper towel, then crumble it finely with a fork or by hand in a large mixing bowl.
2. Cut the Chinese cabbage into very fine strips, then chop finely. Finely slice the white and light green parts of the spring onions (set the green tops aside for serving). Crush the garlic, and peel and finely grate the ginger.
3. Add two thirds of the crushed garlic and grated ginger, the chopped cabbage, spring onion, 1 tablespoon of soy sauce, 1 teaspoon of sesame oil and a pinch of ground black pepper to the crumbled tofu. Mix the filling thoroughly until it holds together slightly.
4. Lay out a wonton wrapper and place a heaped teaspoon of tofu filling in the centre. Lightly moisten the edges of the wrapper with water, fold in half into a half-moon, and seal the edges airtight by pleating or pressing them firmly.
5. In a large pot, bring the vegetable broth to the boil. Add the remaining ginger, garlic, the remaining 1 tablespoon of soy sauce and the remaining sesame oil.
6. When the soup is simmering, carefully lower in the shaped mandu dumplings. Cook over moderate heat for 4-5 minutes until the dumplings float to the surface and the wrappers turn translucent and soft.
7. Ladle the hot soup and tofu dumplings into deep bowls. Sprinkle with the reserved fresh spring onion rings and serve immediately, steaming hot.

## Tips

- The mandu dumplings can be made in advance and frozen on a tray; from frozen they can go straight into the hot broth, with a cooking time only 2 minutes longer.
- For an even richer soup, in the last minute of cooking drizzle a lightly beaten egg into the hot broth while stirring slowly, as is Korean custom.
