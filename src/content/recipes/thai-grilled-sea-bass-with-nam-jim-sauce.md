## Method

1. **Preparation and marinade:** Rinse the sea bass fillets thoroughly with cold water, then pat dry with kitchen paper. Score the skin side shallowly with a sharp knife in 2-3 places, then gently rub with a pinch of fine salt and 1 tablespoon of sunflower oil.
2. Bruise the lemongrass with the back of a heavy knife or a rolling pin so the aromatic oils are released, then chop the tender white part very finely. Also chop one clove of garlic finely and spread it, together with the lemongrass, over the fish.
3. **The spicy nam jim sauce and the rice:** Rinse the jasmine rice thoroughly, then cook it covered over low heat in twice its volume of lightly salted water for 12-14 minutes until tender and fluffy, then leave to rest covered in its steam.
4. For the sauce, chop the remaining two cloves of garlic and the red chilli and crush them in a mortar or food processor. Add the fish sauce, cane sugar and freshly squeezed lime juice. Stir until the sugar dissolves, giving a balanced, spicy sauce.
5. **Cooking and serving:** Heat a grill pan to medium-high with the remaining 1 tablespoon of sunflower oil. Place the sea bass fillets skin side down in the hot pan and cook for 3-4 minutes until the skin is nice and crisp. Then carefully turn them over and cook the flesh side for another 2 minutes until done.
6. Place the crispy fillets on plates with the steamed jasmine rice, drizzle with the spicy lemongrass-chilli sauce, and serve immediately garnished with fresh coriander leaves and lime wedges.

## Tips

- Sea bass skin gets truly crisp if you pat it completely dry with a paper towel before cooking and, once in the hot pan, gently press it down with a spatula for the first 30 seconds so it does not curl up.
- If the outer layer of the lemongrass is woody, remove the first 1-2 layers and use only the tender, lower white-pale green part of the stalk for the delicate seasoning.
