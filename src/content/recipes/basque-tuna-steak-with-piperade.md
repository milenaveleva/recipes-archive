## Method

1. **Preparation:** Wash and seed the kápia and green peppers, then cut them into thin lengthwise strips. Peel the onion and slice it into half-moons, and finely chop the garlic cloves.
2. Take the fresh tuna steaks out of the fridge 15 minutes before cooking so they come to room temperature. Pat off excess moisture thoroughly with paper towels, then season both sides lightly with salt and pepper.
3. **Making the Basque piperade:** In a deeper pan, heat 2 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil over medium heat. Add the sliced onion, kápia pepper and green pepper, and with a pinch of salt cook slowly over low heat for about 10-12 minutes until the peppers soften and take on a sweet aroma.
4. Stir in the finely chopped garlic and smoked paprika, and cook for half a minute until the aroma rises. Add the canned chopped tomatoes and the white wine vinegar, bring to a boil, then cook on low heat for another 8-10 minutes until the ragù becomes thick and juicy. Taste and, if needed, season with salt and freshly ground black pepper.
5. **Searing the tuna steak and serving:** Heat a heavy-bottomed pan (preferably cast iron) until smoking with the remaining 1 tablespoon of extra virgin olive oil. Place the seasoned tuna steaks in it and sear for just 1.5-2 minutes per side so the crust is nicely browned and the centre stays pleasantly pink and juicy.
6. Divide the hot, silky piperade vegetable ragù between the bottoms of two plates. Lay the freshly seared tuna steaks on the vegetable bed, sprinkle with freshly chopped parsley and serve immediately, hot.

## Tips

- Tuna steak is tastiest and juiciest when the inside stays pink (medium-rare). Take care not to overcook it, or the fish can easily dry out.
- The piperade can even be made the day before, as the flavours blend further overnight. Just reheat it before serving while you sear the fish fresh.
