## Method

1. Preheat the oven to 190 °C (180 °C for a fan oven).
2. In a small bowl, mix the olive oil with the garlic, lemon juice, grated lemon zest, salt, pepper and half of the chopped fresh dill.
3. Rinse the pollock fillets, pat dry with a paper towel and place them on a baking tray lined with baking paper. Brush generously on both sides with the lemon-dill mixture.
4. Put in the oven and bake for about 18–20 minutes, until the flesh is white and flakes easily (depending on the thickness of the fillets). For the last 2 minutes turn on the grill function so the top browns lightly.
5. Before serving, sprinkle with the remaining dill and parsley and garnish with lemon slices. For a faster version, fry the fish in a pan in a little olive oil over medium heat for 2–3 minutes on each side until golden.
6. Purée the green peas with a hand blender together with the plant-based cream, the grated garlic and the seasonings. If needed, loosen it to a creamy consistency with 1–2 tablespoons of water or the liquid from the can.
7. Serve the baked fish with the pea purée, cooked rice or quinoa, or a fresh lemony arugula salad.

## Tips

- Dietitian's tip: you can also serve it with rice, pasta suited to your diet, millet, bulgur or couscous; basmati rice is an especially good choice. It has a lower glycaemic index than ordinary white rice, so it raises blood sugar more slowly, keeps you full for longer and is easy to digest. Brown basmati has more fibre, which supports digestion and blood sugar stability even more. It can be a good option for insulin resistance, prediabetes or a mindful diet. Traditional brown rice and coloured (red, black, etc.) rices are also good choices if you want to increase your fibre intake.
- Tip from Mentes Anyu: the more characterful flavour of pollock suits citrusy, herby flavours. If you use frozen fillets, always thaw them and pat off the moisture so the crust becomes really crisp. Source: Mentes Anyu's express cookbook.
